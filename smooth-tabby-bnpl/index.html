What this document is

Tabby lets a customer split a restaurant bill into four interest-free payments. The customer pays the first quarter today; Tabby pays the restaurant the full amount and collects the rest from the customer over the following weeks. The restaurant carries no credit risk.

This document walks through every screen that changes, in the order the work actually happens: you configure it in the admin panel, the merchant sees the results in their panel, and the customer pays with it in the app. Alongside each screen you'll find what we're proposing and why.

Where a choice is genuinely yours to make, it's flagged. Everything flagged is collected again at the end so you have a single list to respond to.

In scope

Tabby as a payment option everywhere Smooth takes money — table QR bills, in-app orders, pay-later, payment links and Foodics tables. Plus Tabby's promotional messaging on the menu, product and cart screens.

Not in scope

Arabic language support, a refund button inside Smooth, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait, and Tabby's mobile app SDKs. Each is explained where it comes up, and none of them block launch.

What we need from you

Answers to the fourteen design questions at the end, and a decision on how Tabby accounts are set up. Everything else is ready to build.

Decisions

The choices that shape everything else

Six decisions define how the whole feature behaves. Each one is set out with what we recommend, why, and what the alternative would cost you.

Each restaurant gets its own Tabby account

Your call — biggest one

Tabby issues credentials per business. We need to decide whether every restaurant on Smooth signs up with Tabby individually, or whether Smooth signs up once and puts all restaurants underneath it.

Our recommendation

One Tabby account per restaurant. You enter each restaurant's Tabby details into the Smooth admin panel, exactly the way AFS credentials already work today.

Why it's the safer choice

Tabby pays each restaurant directly. Smooth never holds anyone else's money, so no payment-institution licensing question arises, and every restaurant sees its own Tabby dashboard and settlements.

The alternative

One Smooth account for everyone. Tabby switches on instantly for every restaurant with no paperwork — but Tabby pays Smooth, and Smooth then has to pay each restaurant. That makes Smooth a money handler, which needs Tabby's approval and probably a licence.

The trade-off

Per-restaurant means onboarding friction: every restaurant has to sign with Tabby before it can be switched on. If you want Tabby live across the whole platform on day one, tell us — we can build it so both models work.

Tabby sits alongside the card gateway, it doesn't replace it

Recommended

Today each restaurant is on exactly one card gateway — Stripe, Telr or AFS — and you tick which methods that gateway offers.

What we're doing

Tabby becomes another tickbox in the same list, next to Apple Pay, Google Pay and Card. It works with all three gateways because Tabby handles the money itself.

Why

Nothing about the existing card setup changes. A restaurant on Telr keeps Telr for cards and simply gains Tabby. One tickbox to turn on, one tickbox to turn off.

What you'll see

In the admin panel, the payment-methods list gains a fifth option: Tabby (Pay in 4). Everywhere Smooth already shows payment methods, Tabby appears automatically.

We ask the customer for their name and email — once

Your call

Tabby requires a name, an email and a mobile number for every payment. Smooth only ever requires a phone number, and guests scanning a QR code have given us nothing at all.

What we're doing

When the customer picks Tabby, a short form appears asking for name, email and mobile. For a signed-in customer these are pre-filled and they just tap Continue. Anything they type is saved to their profile, so they never fill it in twice.

Why

The alternative is forcing customers to create an account before they can use Tabby — which would shut out the guest QR customers who are the majority of Smooth's traffic.

The cost

One extra screen, only for Tabby. Every other payment method stays exactly as fast as it is today.

Tabby appears on every bill — Tabby decides who qualifies

Recommended

Tabby has a minimum and maximum order value, and it also runs its own credit check on each customer. Both can change without notice.

What we're doing

Tabby is offered on full bills, split shares, custom amounts, payment links and Foodics tables alike. When the customer confirms their details, we check with Tabby in the background before sending them anywhere.

If Tabby says no

The customer sees Tabby's own wording — "This purchase is below the minimum required", "This purchase exceeds your spending limit with Tabby", or a general decline — and a button to pick another method. They are never sent to a dead end.

Why not hide it below a threshold

Because we'd have to hard-code Tabby's limits, and the moment Tabby changes them our app is wrong. Letting Tabby answer keeps us permanently in step.

An order is only marked paid once the money is genuinely on its way

Recommended

Tabby works in two steps that cards don't have. First it approves the customer. Then the restaurant has to claim the money. If nobody claims it, it is never paid out.

What we're doing

Smooth claims the money automatically the instant Tabby approves the customer — and only then marks the bill paid, prints the receipt and awards cashback.

Why this order matters

If we marked the bill paid first and claimed later, a failed claim would leave a table showing "settled" while no money ever arrived. This way an order can never look paid when it isn't.

Three safety nets

If the claim doesn't go through immediately, Tabby notifies us and we retry. A background check also sweeps every few minutes for anything stuck. No approved payment is left unclaimed.

Refunds are issued in Tabby's dashboard — Smooth shows them accurately

Your call

Smooth has never had a refund feature for any payment method. Tabby, on the other hand, has a full refund tool in its own merchant dashboard.

What we're doing

The restaurant refunds from Tabby's dashboard. Tabby tells Smooth immediately, and the transaction row in the merchant panel updates to read Refunded AED 62.50 of AED 250.00.

Why not a refund button in Smooth

Building one only for Tabby would leave Stripe, Telr and AFS without it — confusing for merchants. A proper refund feature across all methods is a project in its own right.

What it does guarantee

Smooth's reports are never wrong. A refunded Tabby payment shows as refunded in transactions, dashboards and exports without anyone having to remember to update it.

5 · Tabby's own screens Not ours

The customer moves to Tabby's hosted checkout — in the same browser tab, not a pop-up. That's Tabby's requirement, and it also means nothing is lost if the customer switches apps mid-way.

On Tabby's side they confirm their identity, receive an SMS code and pick a card for the instalments. We don't design or control any of it, and no card details ever touch Smooth.

Tabby then sends them back to Smooth on one of three paths: approved, cancelled, or declined.

A rule we can't bend

Tabby is explicit that a merchant must never trust the return link alone. Smooth always verifies the payment directly with Tabby's servers before settling a bill — so a customer can't fake a successful return.

6 · Back in Smooth New

A brief confirming screen appears while Smooth verifies with Tabby and claims the money — usually a second or two. Then:

  • Approved — straight to the normal receipt screen. Bill settled, cashback awarded, table released. Identical to any other payment.
  • Cancelled — "You cancelled the payment. Nothing was charged," with the payment screen one tap away.
  • Declined — Tabby's own decline wording, plus a prompt to pick another method.

Past Tabby payments carry a Tabby badge in the customer's history, and the emailed and downloaded receipts say Tabby (Pay in 4) with the instalment summary.

Deliberately not shown: the customer's remaining instalments, due dates or balance. Tabby owns that relationship and shows it in the Tabby app. Duplicating it in Smooth would risk showing a stale figure — and it would make Smooth look like the lender, which it isn't.

Tell us if you disagree

Flows

How Tabby behaves with everything Smooth already does

Smooth's payment screen already handles split bills, cashback, tips and payment links. Here's exactly how each behaves once Tabby is in the mix.

Split bill

Four people, four separate Tabby plans

A split bill works exactly as it does today, except each person can independently choose Tabby. If four people split an AED 250 bill, each pays AED 62.50 — and any of them can put their own share on Tabby while the others use Apple Pay or cash. Each Tabby user gets their own Pay-in-4 plan for their own share.

The one thing to know: Tabby has a minimum order value. A small share of a small bill may fall below it, in which case that person sees "The purchase amount is below the minimum required" and simply picks another method. The rest of the table is unaffected.

SituationWhat the customer seesWhat the merchant sees
2 of 4 people pay with Tabby at the same timeBoth succeed, each with their own planTwo Tabby rows, correct running balance
A share falls under Tabby's minimumTabby's minimum-amount message and another method suggestedNothing — no failed payment is recorded
Someone settles the whole bill while another is mid-Tabby"This bill has already been settled. You have not been charged."One payment for the full bill; no duplicate
The case we've designed hardest for

That last row is the one that would otherwise double-charge someone. If a bill is already settled by the time a Tabby customer returns, Smooth cancels the Tabby plan instead of claiming the money — Tabby refunds them in full, automatically, and they're told clearly that nothing was taken.

Smooth Cashback

Cashback works with Tabby in both directions

Spending cashback. If a customer applies AED 40 of Smooth cashback to a AED 250 bill, Tabby is asked for AED 210 — and the instalments shown become 4 × AED 52.50. The discount is applied before Tabby ever sees the amount.

Earning cashback. A Tabby payment earns Smooth cashback on exactly the same terms as a card payment. From the loyalty engine's point of view Tabby is just another way the bill got paid.

Paying entirely with cashback. Unchanged — if cashback covers the whole bill there's nothing left to finance, so Tabby isn't offered.

Confirm with us

Should a Tabby payment earn cashback at the same rate as a card payment? Tabby's fees are typically higher, so you may want a different rate — or none. We've assumed the same rate; changing it is easy now and awkward later.

Tips

The tip is financed along with the bill

A tip is added before Tabby is asked for the amount, so a AED 250 bill with a AED 25 tip becomes a AED 275 Tabby plan — 4 × AED 68.75. The restaurant receives the tip in full with the rest of the bill.

Where a payment link has tips switched off, nothing changes: no tip, no effect on Tabby.

Payment links, pay-later & Foodics

All three get Tabby with no extra work

  • Payment links — a customer opening a link sees Tabby in the methods list and the instalment figure under the payable amount, just as on a table bill.
  • Pay later — a customer who eats first and pays afterwards can choose Tabby at the end. No difference.
  • Foodics tables — Tabby works on Foodics bills too. Foodics bills can change while the customer is paying, so if the total drops mid-payment Smooth claims only what's actually owed and cancels the remainder, which Tabby refunds automatically.
Refunds

How a Tabby refund actually happens

01Restaurant refundsIn Tabby's own merchant dashboard, full or partial
02Tabby adjustsThe customer's remaining instalments are reduced or cancelled
03Smooth updatesTabby notifies us within seconds; the transaction row updates itself

The customer is refunded by Tabby directly and sees it in the Tabby app. The restaurant sees the refund reflected in Smooth without touching anything.

Known limitation, and it's a real one

If a refunded bill had earned Smooth cashback, that cashback is not automatically taken back. Smooth has never had refund handling for any payment method, so there's nothing to hook into. If clawback matters to you, it needs to be its own piece of work covering every method — not just Tabby.

Resilience

When something goes wrong

Payments fail in ordinary ways — a customer closes the app, a network drops, someone else pays first. Here's what happens in each case, in plain terms.

What happensWhat the customer seesWhat Smooth does behind the scenes
Customer closes the browser right after approving Nothing at that moment. When they reopen the bill it already shows as paid, with the receipt available. Tabby notifies us independently of the customer's device. We claim the money and settle the bill without them.
Tabby approves but the claim doesn't go through A "confirming your payment" screen for a moment longer than usual, then the receipt. We retry immediately, then every few minutes. The bill is never marked paid until the claim succeeds — so it can't show settled with no money behind it.
Someone else pays the bill first "This bill has already been settled. You have not been charged." We cancel the Tabby plan rather than claiming it. Tabby refunds in full, automatically. No double charge.
The Foodics bill drops while the customer is on Tabby The receipt shows the corrected, lower amount. We claim only what's owed and cancel the rest; Tabby refunds the difference to the customer.
Tabby's service is unavailable "Tabby is unavailable right now. Please try again or choose another method." Tabby stays visible in the list. Nothing is recorded, nothing is charged. Every other payment method carries on working normally.
Customer taps Pay twice, or the network retries One payment, one receipt. Every Tabby payment carries a unique reference. A repeat is recognised as the same payment, not a new one.
Customer abandons Tabby's checkout Back on the payment screen with the bill untouched. They can try Tabby again or pick another method. The abandoned attempt expires on Tabby's side. Nothing is charged and nothing is recorded against the bill.
Action required

Decisions we need from you

Each has our recommendation next to it, so a simple "agreed" is a complete answer. Only tell us where you want something different.

01
Should each restaurant have its own Tabby account, or should Smooth hold one account for all of them?

We recommend one account per restaurant. Tabby then pays each restaurant directly and Smooth never handles anyone else's money. A single Smooth account would switch Tabby on everywhere instantly, but makes Smooth responsible for paying restaurants out — which needs Tabby's approval and most likely a licence.

02
Where should Tabby sit in the payment-methods list?

We recommend second — under Smooth Cashback, above Apple Pay. It's new, so it needs to be seen. The pre-selected method doesn't change, so the express Apple Pay path stays exactly as fast.

03
Is the extra "your details" screen acceptable, or should Tabby require a Smooth account?

We recommend the short form. Requiring an account would shut out guest QR customers, who are the majority of Smooth's traffic. The form is one screen and only appears for Tabby.

04
Should guests — customers who aren't signed in — be able to use Tabby at all?

We recommend yes. They give their details on the form and Tabby does its own identity checks regardless. Restricting Tabby to signed-in customers would cut its reach sharply.

05
On the menu screen: a plain Tabby strip, or Tabby's priced widget?

We recommend the plain strip. A menu has dozens of prices and no single one to quote. The priced widget goes on the dish and cart screens, where the price is real and updates live.

06
Should Tabby be offered on split bills and custom amounts, or only on full bills?

We recommend offering it everywhere. Tabby applies its own minimum and tells the customer directly if a share is too small. Hiding it ourselves would mean hard-coding Tabby's limits and going stale the moment they change.

07
Should a Tabby payment earn Smooth cashback at the same rate as a card payment?

We've assumed yes. Tabby's merchant fee is typically higher than a card's, so you may want a reduced rate — or none — for Tabby. This is far easier to set now than to change later.

08
Should merchants be able to switch Tabby on and off themselves?

We recommend admin-only, matching how the card gateway works today. Tabby credentials are sensitive and a wrong entry breaks payments, so it belongs with the team that already handles gateway setup.

09
In merchant reports, should Tabby be its own line or grouped with card payments?

We recommend its own line. Tabby's fees and settlement timing differ from cards, so a restaurant reconciling its bank account needs to see them separately.

10
Are you comfortable with refunds being issued in Tabby's dashboard rather than in Smooth?

We recommend yes for now. Smooth's reports stay accurate automatically. Building a refund button only for Tabby would leave Stripe, Telr and AFS without one — a proper cross-method refund feature is its own project.

11
Should we automatically save the name and email a customer types for Tabby to their Smooth profile?

We recommend yes. It means they only ever type it once. If you'd prefer an explicit "save my details" tick, we'll add one.

12
When Tabby declines a customer, should we suggest a specific alternative or just reopen the method list?

We've assumed reopening the list with Tabby's own explanation shown. We can instead push them straight to a named method — say Apple Pay — if you'd rather reduce the taps.

13
Should customers see their remaining Tabby instalments inside Smooth?

We recommend not. Tabby owns that relationship and shows it in the Tabby app; mirroring it risks showing stale figures and makes Smooth look like the lender. Say the word if you want it and we'll scope it.

14
Is Arabic in scope for this release?

We've assumed not. The Smooth customer app is English-only today with no Arabic support anywhere, so adding it for Tabby alone isn't possible. Tabby's own checklist asks for Arabic, so we're raising it with them — but it would be a separate project across the whole app.
