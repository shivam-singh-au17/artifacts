The product

Who is involved, and what each of them wants

Four parties. Everything in this document is one of them doing one thing.

PartyWhat they wantWhere they do it
Our team
Smooth staff		 Bring a business on board, connect their gateway, and make sure no payment is ever lost. Admin CMS, a new internal panel. Screens A-01 to A-16.
The business
our customer		 Take card payments on their own website with as little work as possible, and see their money. Merchant CMS, a new panel they log into. Screens M-01 to M-12.
The shopper

The gateway

The single most important idea

The business connects to us, never to a gateway. Which gateway sits behind them is our setting, and we can change it later without them touching a single line of their code. Every screen in this document is built so that stays true.

Why the website, not the business

Because a business may run two shops on two different gateways, and because we want to move one of them to a new gateway without touching the other. If keys and gateways hung off the business, neither would be possible.

What the business shares

Only their company details, their staff logins, and their relationship with us. Nothing that a payment depends on.

What this means for the panels

Almost every screen in the merchant panel needs a website selector at the top. A business with one website will never notice it. A business with two will need it everywhere.

2.1 One payment, and everything hanging off it

A shopper may try twice: once with a card that gets declined, then with Apple Pay that works. That is one payment with two tries, not two payments. Keeping those separate is what lets the merchant see "this order is paid" while our team can still see "the first try was declined by the bank, here is why".

What we hold about a single payment

2.2 If this changes, what else changes

This table is the whole product on one page. It is also the quickest way to check whether a screen is designed correctly: if a change made on that screen would surprise somebody, that screen needs a warning on it.

If this changesThis is affectedThis is deliberately not affected
The gateway behind a website Only payments started after the switch go to the new gateway. Apple Pay also has to be set up again, because Apple ties a web address to the payment company that registered it, and the file on their server belongs to that company. Their keys, their code, their notification URL, their address list, card payments, Google Pay, and every payment already taken. This is the entire point of the product. Apple Pay is the single exception, and the switch screen says so before anything is confirmed.
A secret key is revoked Any server still using that key can no longer create payments, immediately. Payments already created, and every other key. This is exactly why several live keys are allowed at once.
An address is removed The checkout box stops appearing on that address. Their other addresses, and payments already taken from the removed one.
Apple verification is lost The Apple Pay button stops appearing on that address, by itself, within a day. Card payments and Google Pay, which keep working normally.
The business is suspended Every website under it stops accepting new payments. All records, and payments already in flight, which still complete and are still reported.
A website goes live Live keys appear for the first time, and the test ribbon disappears from the box once a live key is used. Test mode, which keeps working forever afterwards.
The notification secret is rotated New notifications are signed with the new secret. Nothing breaks. The old secret keeps verifying for 24 hours, so they can deploy at their own pace.
Branding is changed The next checkout box shows the new name, logo and colour. Everything else. Branding can never affect whether a payment works.
03
The flow

How a payment actually moves

Seven steps, four parties. Worth reading once slowly, because every screen in the rest of this document is a window onto one of these steps.

One successful payment, start to finish

                    this is the moment they ship the goods
   sees "paid"      ◄── the screen is only a courtesy
Rule one

The amount comes from their server, at step 2. The shopper's browser never tells us a price. So a shopper who edits the page cannot pay less, and there is no route through which they could try.

Rule two

Step 7 is the truth, not the screen. The message on the shopper's screen is display only. The business releases the goods when their own server receives our notification. We say this in the documentation, on the install screen, and inside the notification itself, because every integrator tries to ship on the screen at least once.

Rule three

Card details never touch two of the four parties. At step 5 they travel from the shopper's device straight to the gateway. Not to the business's servers, and not to ours. This is the single most important design decision in the product.

3.1 The three places it can go wrong

What happensWhat is actually trueWhat we do about it
The bank says no No money moved The shopper sees a plain reason and a button to try another card. The business is told the attempt failed. Nothing is left stuck.
The shopper closes the tab right after paying They were charged This is the dangerous one. Our screen is gone, so nothing in the browser can tell us anything. The system keeps asking the gateway by itself until it gets a final answer, then records the payment and notifies the business exactly as normal. The shopper is charged once, and the order is not lost.
The gateway does not answer us We do not know We never guess. The payment is marked "checking", never "failed", and the system keeps asking. Marking it failed is precisely how a charged customer ends up paying a second time.
The idea behind all three rows

"We do not know" is never the same as "it failed". A payment that is stuck can always be resolved later. A payment we wrongly declared failed leads to a customer paying twice, and that cannot be undone quietly. Every screen in the admin panel carrying the words "checking" or "at risk" exists because of this one sentence.

Language

The words on every screen

Three short lists. If the product uses these words consistently, nobody has to be trained to read it.

4.1 What a payment's status means

The one exception, and it matters

An expired payment can still become paid. If a shopper started the bank's code step at minute 29 and finished at minute 31, the bank really did take the money. Refusing to record it would leave a customer charged with nothing to show for it. So the clock stops new attempts, and it never throws away real money. This is the only status in the product that can move backwards, and this is the entire reason it can.

4.2 What we record about every payment

Set out in plain language because it decides what the screens can show, what the reports can answer, and what we can prove if a payment is ever disputed.

Their side
  • The amount and currency
  • Their own order reference
  • A description, if they sent one
  • Any extra labels they attached, returned to them untouched
  • Who the customer was, if they told us: a reference, name, email, phone
What happened
  • Every try, with its own result and time
  • The method used: card, Apple Pay or Google Pay
  • Card brand, first six and last four digits, expiry
  • Whether the bank asked for a code
  • Why it failed, in our own words rather than the gateway's
  • Which website address the box was shown on
Our proof
  • Which gateway account took it, locked forever
  • The gateway's own references, including the bank's authorisation code and the reference that appears in the monthly settlement report
  • What the gateway said it took, next to what we asked for
  • Every message the gateway sent us, stored as received
  • Every notification we sent, and their server's answer
Why the third column exists

Those gateway references are what turn the monthly settlement check from guesswork into an exact match. In the current Smooth Pay system they arrive on every payment and are only written to a log, which is why today's reconciliation has to match on merchant, amount and time, and sometimes cannot tell two similar payments apart. Storing them costs one column each and removes that whole problem before it starts.

What we never record, anywhere, in any form

The full card number, the three digits on the back, or anything from which they could be reconstructed. Not in the database, not in a log, not in an error report, not in a support tool. No screen in this product could show them, because the data never arrives in the first place.

4.3 What we tell the business, and when

We sendWhenWhat they should do
PaidThe moment money is confirmedShip the goods. This is the only message they must act on
Attempt failedA try was declinedNothing. Useful for their own reporting
ExpiredNobody paid in timeRelease any stock they were holding

  • Every message carries a unique id, and the same message may arrive twice. Their server should ignore one it has already seen. This is normal for every payment provider, and it is stated in bold in the documentation.
  • Messages about one payment always arrive in order.
  • Every message is signed, so they can be certain it came from us and not from somebody pretending to be us.
  • If their server is down we keep trying for about two days, and they can see every attempt on their own screen.
05
Who gets told what

Every message the product sends

Five different channels carry information in this product, and each one is trusted differently. Getting these confused is the most common way a payment integration goes wrong, so they are separated here before any screen is designed.

5.1 The five channels

ChannelFrom, toCarriesHow much it can be trusted
1. Page message
IN THE BROWSER		 Our box to the business's own page The box is ready, the box changed height, the payment succeeded, failed or was cancelled Display only. Good enough to show a spinner or move the shopper on. Never good enough to release goods, because anything in a browser can be faked or simply never arrive
2. Live status
BOX TO US		 Our box to our own server "Has this payment finished yet?" Accurate while the tab is open. It dies the instant the shopper closes the tab, which is exactly why channel 5 exists
3. Server notification
THE IMPORTANT ONE		 Our server to the business's server Paid, attempt failed, expired, cancelled, on hold This is the truth. Signed by us, retried for two days, and the only message a business should ever act on
4. Email
TO PEOPLE		 Us to humans, on both sides Invitations, go-live decisions, security notices, "your notification endpoint is failing" Informational. No part of a payment ever depends on an email arriving
5. Our own chasing
THE SAFETY NET		 Our server to the gateway, by itself "What actually happened to this payment?" The final word. It runs whether or not any browser is open and whether or not the gateway ever told us anything
The sentence that summarises all five

Channels 1 and 2 make the shopper's screen feel right. Channels 3 and 5 make the money right. If every browser in the world failed at once, channels 3 and 5 would still deliver every payment correctly, late but complete. That is the test of whether this design is sound.

One deliberate absence

We send the shopper nothing. No receipt, no confirmation, no email at all. The shopper bought from the business, not from us, and a message from a name they do not recognise would create support calls for both sides. The business sends the receipt, from their own system, as they already do today. This is a decision, and it is listed in §15 so the client can overturn it if they want to.

How long it should take

Steps 1 to 4 are ours and take minutes. Steps 5 to 8 are theirs and should take under an hour with a developer available. Step 6 is the only one with an outside wait, because Apple has to check their file.

What never happens

The business never sees the word entity, never sees a gateway name in an error message, and never has to contact the gateway. If a gateway question comes up, it comes to us.

What we still do by hand

Getting them an account with the gateway itself. That is paperwork between them, us and the gateway, exactly as it works today. Everything after the credentials arrive is a screen in this document.

A-08 Change gateway

The screen that makes the whole product worth building. It moves one website from one gateway to another. The business changes nothing.

It is deliberately a screen with warnings on it, not a dropdown, because three things are true and easy to forget:

  • Payments already in progress finish on the old gateway. Nothing is interrupted, so there is no need to pick a quiet hour.
  • The old connection is switched off, never deleted. Refunds and monthly settlement checks for old payments still need it.
  • Saved cards, when we have them, do not move. This screen says so plainly, with the count of affected customers, before the switch is confirmed.

The confirmation requires typing the website address, the same way a dangerous action should.

The card fields are not ours. They are supplied by the gateway inside their own secure frame, and we wrap them in our design. Phase 1 addition

Apple Pay and Google Pay: what it really takes

These two were added to Phase 1 at the client's request. They are worth it, because on a phone they convert far better than a card form. But they are not a switch we can flip alone, and the product has to be honest about that.

Apple Pay

Apple only allows the Apple Pay button on a website it has checked in advance. Because our box sits inside the business's page, it is the business's own web address that Apple checks, not ours.

So the business has to put one small file on their own server, at a fixed path, and we then ask Apple to verify it. Screen M-04 exists entirely for this.

Until it is verified, we do not render the button.

One more rule of Apple's, and it is the one with consequences. Apple lets several businesses share a web address, but it does not let the same web address be registered by two different payment companies. The file sits at one fixed path, and it belongs to whichever gateway put it there. So Apple Pay is tied to the gateway that set it up, and moving a business to a different gateway means doing this step again.

Google Pay

No file to host, which makes it the easier of the two. It needs a Google merchant identifier on the gateway account, and for real payments the business has to be registered on Google's side.

It also needs the browser to allow payment inside our box, which our own snippet takes care of automatically.

What we handle silently

Our snippet creates the box with the browser permission that both wallets require. The business does not need to know this exists, and cannot get it wrong, because they never write the box themselves.

If a business builds their own box by hand instead of using our snippet, wallets will not appear. The documentation says so in one line.

Must be confirmed with the gateway before this part is built

Today each business has its own Apple merchant identifier through the gateway. Before we write the Apple Pay part of the onboarding screen, we need the gateway to tell us in writing who registers the business's web address with Apple: whether they do it for us through Apple's registration interface for payment providers, or whether each business has to be taken through Apple's own portal. The answer decides whether M-04 is a one-click screen or a guided, slower process. The rest of Phase 1 does not depend on this answer, so it does not block the other work.

15.1 What we recommend on each, so nobody has to guess

The questions above are ours to raise. The answers below are ours to propose. Nobody needs to research a payment gateway to move this forward: read our recommendation, and either say yes or tell us where you disagree. Where the answer has to come from the gateway rather than from us, we have designed so that waiting for it does not stop the work.

#We recommendWhy this oneWhat happens while we wait
1 Build for the harder case: the business hosts one file on their own site, and we verify it. And record that verification against the gateway it was obtained through, not against the address on its own Apple checks the file on the business's own website whichever route we take, so that step exists either way. If the gateway turns out to register domains for us, our screen simply becomes one click instead of three steps. Designing for the easier case first would mean rebuilding the screen if the answer is the harder one.

Recording it against the gateway matters for the same reason: Apple will not let two payment companies register one web address, so a business moved to a different gateway has to do this step again. Storing it that way makes the switch screen able to warn them instead of the button quietly disappearing.		 Nothing stops. Screen M-04 is built as the guided version, and the registration call itself is one small piece that is switched on when the gateway answers.
2 Ask, but do not depend on it. Test the wallets against our own simulator, and do the real device test with one small live payment on our own account. Sandboxes are frequently missing wallet support, and finding that out in week six would be expensive. The platform already uses a small live test payment for gateway approval, so the practice exists. Nothing stops. The wallet work is placed late in the schedule for exactly this reason.
3 Do not let our tests depend on the gateway's sandbox at all. Build our own simulator that can be told to succeed, decline, ask for a bank code, or go silent. This is the single most useful decision on this list. Our automated tests then run in seconds, always, whether the sandbox is up, down or behaving differently this week. The real sandbox is used only for the final manual check. Nothing stops. Only the printed list of test card numbers on M-07 waits for the gateway, and that is a text change on one screen.
4 Design so that one address works for both test and live. Our system works out which is which from the message itself. Every payment we create carries our own reference, and that reference tells us the business, the website and whether it was test or live. So we do not need the gateway to keep them apart for us. If it turns out it can, we use that as an extra layer rather than as a requirement. Nothing stops, and a real weakness in the current platform is avoided, where gateway messages sometimes arrive at the wrong endpoint and are silently dropped.
5 Do not ask. Handle both. Read every incoming message as plain text first, decrypt it if it is encrypted, and carry on. Half a day of work, and the existing platform already has the decryption code to copy. After that, the answer to the question genuinely does not matter, which is better than a confident answer. Nothing stops.
6 One business, with its websites underneath it. Not a separate business per website. This is the only question that truly needs a decision from your side, and here is why it matters in plain terms. If each website is its own business in our system, then a company with two shops gets two logins, two dashboards, two sets of paperwork, and can never see their total money in one place. If instead the websites sit under one business, all of that is solved, and a company with only one website never even sees the extra level, because we hide it.

And the cost of being wrong runs one way only. Adding a grouping level later means moving live payment records under a new parent, which is the kind of change nobody wants to make on real money. Having the level and not needing it costs nothing.		 This one we would like answered first. Six tables and every access check are shaped by it.
7 Yes, exactly as today: each business keeps its own gateway account and the money goes straight to them. It is what you have already confirmed, it needs no licence, and it is the model the whole design assumes. We have also built the setting that will one day let the money come to us instead, and we recommend deliberately blocking that setting in code until the Central Bank licence is actually in hand. That way nobody can switch it on by accident before we are allowed to operate that way. Nothing stops. This is the status quo.
8 We write the statement, and have it checked by someone qualified before the first business asks for it. Any business taking card payments has a yearly compliance form to fill in. Whether they can use the short, easy version depends on us confirming, in writing, what protections our payment box carries. Handing them that statement at onboarding turns a difficult conversation into a formality, and makes us noticeably easier to buy from than a gateway that leaves them to work it out. Nothing stops. It is needed before the first real business signs, not before code starts.
9 No. The business sends the receipt, from their own system, as they do today. The shopper bought from the business, not from us. An email arriving from a name they do not recognise produces "who has charged me?" support calls for both sides, and at worst gets reported as a scam. The business also knows what was actually ordered, where we only know an amount.

If a business later asks us to send it, we can offer that in a later phase, sent under their name and with their reply address, never ours.		 Nothing stops, and it removes a whole module from the first version.
10 Thirty minutes, and adjustable per website. Shorter than about fifteen minutes and we start losing real sales: a shopper who walks off to find their wallet, or whose bank code is slow to arrive, comes back to a dead page. Much longer and the business is holding stock for a sale that will never happen. Thirty minutes comfortably covers a bank verification step and is what most gateways settle on.

Worth knowing: a shorter window is not a money risk, only a sales one. If the bank really did take the money at the last second, we still record the payment. Expiry stops new attempts; it never throws away real money.		 Nothing stops. It is one number, and a business selling something with tight stock can be given a shorter one.
The point of the table above

With these recommendations in place, only question 6 actually needs a decision before work starts, and that decision is a yes or a correction, not research. Everything else is either already answered by us, or has been designed so that waiting on the gateway costs us nothing. That is deliberate: a plan that stalls every time an outside party is slow to reply is not a plan.
