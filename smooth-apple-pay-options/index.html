Apple Pay for Geidea restaurants: the options

Why we need a decision

Apple allows only one payment company to run Apple Pay on one website address. Our customer website (app.smoothpay.ai) is already set up for AFS, and Apple does not allow Geidea to be added to the same address.

We want Geidea restaurants’ customers to pay with Apple Pay on our own page, exactly as they do today, without being sent to another company’s page. There are three ways to do that.

Option A: A second Smooth Pay web address for Geidea restaurants (recommended)

How it works

  • AFS restaurants keep working exactly as today on app.smoothpay.ai.
  • Geidea restaurants use a second Smooth Pay address, for example pay.smoothpay.ai.
  • When a customer scans a Geidea restaurant’s QR code, they are moved to the second address automatically, in the blink of an eye. The app looks the same, the steps are the same, and Apple Pay is on the same screen.

What changes

  • Restaurants: nothing. QR codes stay the same, with no reprinting.
  • Customers: the address at the top of the browser is different at Geidea restaurants. A customer who was signed in has to sign in once more on the new address. We can remove this with some extra work.
  • Smooth Pay: we set up the second address, and Geidea checks and approves it.

Why we recommend it

  • It does not touch the restaurants that are live on AFS today, so there is no risk to them.
  • It is the setup Apple itself recommends.
  • It does not need AFS’s permission.
  • Moving a restaurant between AFS and Geidea later happens automatically, with no reprinting.

Option B: Smooth Pay gets its own Apple Pay account, with one address for everyone

How it works

  • Smooth Pay opens its own Apple Pay account.
  • Both AFS and Geidea then run Apple Pay through Smooth Pay’s account, on the same address app.smoothpay.ai.

What changes

  • Customers: nothing.
  • Restaurants: nothing visible. But before each Geidea restaurant goes live, we do a setup step with Geidea for that restaurant, and repeat it about every two years.
  • Smooth Pay: AFS must agree in writing to run Apple Pay on Smooth Pay’s account. Then all live AFS restaurants move over at the same moment, in one planned switch.

The risk: on switch day, if anything goes wrong, Apple Pay stops for all AFS restaurants until it is fixed. We cannot start without AFS’s written agreement.

Option C: Smooth Pay handles Apple Pay itself

How it works

  • Smooth Pay opens its own Apple Pay account, and our system processes the Apple Pay payment itself.
  • It then hands the payment to whichever payment company the restaurant uses (AFS, Geidea or a future one).

What changes

  • Customers: nothing.
  • Restaurants: nothing, and no setup per restaurant. Any future payment company can be added easily.
  • Smooth Pay: we must pass a card-security certification (an official security audit that is renewed every year). Both AFS and Geidea must agree to accept payments this way, and live AFS restaurants go through the same one-time switch as in Option B.

The risk: it takes the longest to get ready, and it has the same switch-day risk as Option B.

What is not possible:

  • A separate Apple Pay set-up per restaurant on the same address.
  • Keeping both AFS’s and Geidea’s Apple Pay on the same address.
  • Switching between them back and forth. Apple would turn off the other company’s Apple Pay.

Our recommendation: start with Option A. It is safe for all live restaurants and gives Geidea restaurants Apple Pay on our own page. If later you want everyone on one address, we can move to Option B or C, but only after AFS agrees in writing.

Testing: whichever option you choose, we will test Apple Pay with a small real payment on a Smooth Pay test restaurant before any real restaurant uses it.

What we need from you

  1. Which option you prefer.
  2. If Option A: the name of the second address.
  3. If Option A: whether customers should simply sign in again there, or we build the automatic sign-in (extra work).
